# MarketMantri F&O evolution cards — source boundaries

## Purpose

These two cards explain the product sequence and risk boundary in the MarketMantri investor narrative. They are educational visuals, not trade instructions, return projections, or a claim that a derivative position is inherently safe.

## Source-grounded statements

| Visual statement | Source boundary |
| --- | --- |
| A futures contract is a standardized exchange-traded contract to buy or sell an underlying product at a pre-determined price on a future date. | SEBI Investor, “Understanding derivatives” |
| An option gives the buyer a right, not an obligation; its buyer pays a premium. | SEBI Investor, “Understanding derivatives” |
| Derivatives are primarily used for hedging / minimizing price risk, but are also used for speculation and arbitrage. | SEBI Investor, “Understanding derivatives” |
| A smaller payable amount compared with the underlying price can multiply gains and losses; a wrong speculative view can wipe out net worth. | SEBI Investor, “Understanding derivatives” |
| NSE launched Nifty index futures on 12 June 2000, index options on 4 June 2001, individual-stock options on 2 July 2001, and individual-stock futures on 9 November 2001. | NSE, “About Equity Derivatives” (updated 11 Aug 2026) |

## Card wording rules

1. The timeline is a simple product-evolution explanation, not a claim that cash equity had no earlier history or that futures/options arrived in every Indian venue on a single date.
2. “Low upfront capital” is written as a risk warning: lower premium/margin does **not** mean lower exposure or a safety guarantee.
3. The F&O reality card must be calm and constructive: purpose, leverage/cost reality, preparation checklist, and a bridge into MarketMantri’s research/paper-evidence approach.
4. Do not imply that retail losses automatically become institutional profit, that SEBI is a counterparty, or that market participants possess undisclosed information.
